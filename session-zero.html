The First Rule of Anavale

Color Is Life

In Anavale, color is not decoration. It is health, magic, and vitality all at once — a living measure of how much of the Gigglegloom a person, place, or creature carries. Think of it less like paint and more like the glow that living things have.

Vivid
Fully saturated. The Gigglegloom is here and healthy. Magic flows freely.
Muted
Something is off. Not dangerous yet — but noticeable to those who know.
Faded — Stage 1
Early Dimming. Reversible. Joy accessible but muted. Creatures begin to quiet.
Dimmed — Stage 2
Grey. Joy only remembered, not felt. This is where the Bumble Frogs go silent.
In D&D termsColor loss can affect what magic works and how. Areas of Fading may weaken Gigglegloom spells — or make them unpredictable.
For your characterYour color is part of who you are. It can shift. Protecting it — in others and yourself — is what heroes do here.
What to watch forCreatures change. Frogs go quiet. Fluffets bring grey acorns. These are signs you'll learn to read.

Things You Will Meet

The Merry

These are only four of the dozens of creatures in Anavale. You will meet many more.
Fluffets
Tiny squirrels. Say "bwip" and mean it sincerely. Will bring you an acorn. Will not stop until you accept it.
Bumble Frogs
Hum constantly. Harmonize accidentally with any nearby music. Cannot match pitch. Somehow always sound right.
Tinywings
Thumb-sized dragons. Cannot fly straight. Very proud. Will argue about this at length.
Bounce Beetles
Navigate by bouncing. Make a satisfying "boing." Travel entirely by accident. Always arrive correctly.

Harming a creature unprovoked is not illegal — it is simply something the entire continent considers unforgivable. Enforced accordingly.

The Rules (The Ones That Matter Tonight)

How This Works

D&D has hundreds of rules. You need these.
1
Describe what your character wants to do. That is your entire job. My job is to figure out how the rules handle it. You will never be wrong for trying something.
2
Uncertain outcomes roll the d20. Higher is better. Your abilities (Strength, Dexterity, Wisdom, etc.) and proficiency add to that number. Advantage means roll twice and take the higher. Disadvantage means take the lower.
3
Combat is turn-by-turn. On your turn: move somewhere, do one main thing (attack, cast, help someone), and optionally one small bonus thing. You have hit points — don't worry about what happens at zero yet.
4
Story wins over rules. Always. This is Anavale's first law. If a rule would make a moment worse, we ignore it. You are here to live a story, not pass a test. Anything you're unsure about — just ask.
5
"Yes, and." Whatever another player does, build on it. D&D is collaborative improv. There are no wrong moves — only interesting consequences.

Two Modes

Roleplay vs. Casual Chat

You don't need to be in character the whole time. Switch freely. If you're not sure which mode we're in — just ask.

🎭 Roleplay mode
Speak as your character. Third person is fine: "Elara looks at the guard and says…"
Or just speak directly: "I don't think that's the whole story."

No performance required. Just intention.
💬 Casual mode
Talking as yourself. "Wait, what did that merchant say again?" or "Can I even do that?" or "I am so confused right now."

Out-of-character jokes are welcomed. The table is always safe.
A Special Reward

Inspiration

The game's way of saying: that was exactly right.
✦ What earns Inspiration
Acting on a flaw at the wrong moment — and doing it anyway because that's who your character is
Choosing the kind thing when the clever thing was right there and easier
A roleplay moment so specific it surprises the table — or makes everyone go quiet
Connecting your backstory to what's happening right now, unprompted
Doing what your character would do, even when it costs you
Saying something so perfectly in-character that everyone else immediately agrees that's exactly what they would have said
✗ What doesn't earn it
Clever tactics alone
A lucky roll
Doing what's obviously best for the party
Winning a fight
Anything that isn't about who your character is as a person
Being the one who figures out a puzzle (that's great — but it's not Inspiration)
How to use it Spend before any single d20 roll. Roll twice. Take the higher result. You can only hold one at a time — if you already have one and I offer another, it goes to someone else.

You will not always notice when you've earned it. I will. That's part of my job.
A Gift to Future You

Take Notes

In six weeks you will not remember the innkeeper's name. Future you will be very grateful to present you.
People
Name, where you met them, one word for their vibe. That's all you need. The details come back when you read those three things.
"Pressa Vorn — Wanderkeep station, north bank — tired, trustworthy, definitely knows more than she's saying"
Places
Name, what's there, why it might matter. Don't write everything. Write the thing that felt important when you were there.
"Gobblewump Crossing — river junction, ancient bridge, Gerald lives here, Formery office predates the town"
Things I Don't Understand Yet
The most important column. Hunches, strange details, things that gave you a feeling. The feeling is usually right.
"Why did that merchant look scared when someone mentioned the word Drak? Nobody explained that."

You don't need to write everything. Write the things that felt weird.

Before You Build Them

A Question for You

Think about the character you want to play. Not their stats — their story.
Were they known?
Famous, notorious, beloved by one village, feared by one person? Did the world know their name — or was it better that it didn't?
Were they changed?
Did they find something they were looking for? Lose something they thought they had to keep? Did they become someone different — and was that good?
What did they leave behind?
A thing, a place, a person, a story someone else will tell? A bridge they rebuilt, a wrong they righted, a creature they made sure was safe?

You don't need answers. Hold the question while you build.
The choices will start to feel different.

Your First In-World Moment

The Intake Form

The Formery has an office at every bridge, crossing, and waypoint in Anavale. When your character arrives at Gobblewump Crossing, a clerk will hand you this form. This is yours to fill out.
What is the Formery?
The oldest organization in Anavale. Predates the current bridge, the town, and possibly the calendar. Nobody knows what its original purpose was. It processes forms. Its clerks are always polite. They have seen everything before.

When you arrive in Gobblewump Crossing, a clerk will hand you Form 6-E: Intent Uncertain — Please Clarify Before Crossing.
The Game
1
Fill it out before Session One — in character, as the person you're going to play
2
Bring it (or send it) to me before we start
3
The clerk at the Formery will read your answers back to you at the start of Session One — gravely, without irony
4
Your answers become part of the world. Choose carefully. Or don't.

Session One · Something Is Wrong With Gerald

Three travelers. Three roads. All of them led here.

You arrive at Gobblewump Crossing — the most trafficked river junction in Caparia — each for your own reasons. Maybe you are heading somewhere. Maybe you are leaving something behind. Maybe someone sent you.

None of you planned to stay.

But the town is quieter than it should be.
And the river sounds wrong.

Gerald is a Bumble Frog of extraordinary size and age. He has lived under the bridge longer than the town has had a name.

Every traveler who has passed through in living memory has heard him at dawn — that low harmonic hum that somehow makes whatever you were carrying feel slightly lighter than before.

Three weeks ago, Gerald stopped humming.

He is still there — still large, still under the bridge. But his glow is almost gone.

The Bumble Frogs along the riverbank have gone quiet too, one by one, following his lead into silence.

This morning, someone found a grey acorn on the bridge railing. Left there by a Fluffet whose fur has begun to wash out.

The first grey acorn anyone has seen at Gobblewump Crossing.

The Wanderkeep station keeper, Pressa Vorn, sent word north ten days ago. No response.

The Formery has filed Form 8-R (Report of Unusual Quiet, Creature, Non-Domestic).
They are awaiting processing.

The townspeople talk about it every night at the inn.
Nobody knows what to do.

No one is coming to help.

That is where you arrive.
